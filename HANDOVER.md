## Version 3, branch `v3/carry-transition`: one cloth

An experiment cut from `v3/main` on 4 November 2026. The red fabric flying behind the page and the Carry the Story curtain are one length of satin in two states (`impact/scripts/fabric.js`). The first choreography drew the cloth into the keystone and unfurled it out again; the owner found it quick and unsoft, and the frame-by-frame study (the fabric's `scrub` control) showed the thread leaving the screen. The second settles the cloth in place: on the press the page steps back and the fabric's fixed layer comes forward (`.fabric-layer.is-front`, beneath the header and the curtain's words); then a wave runs along the cloth from its held end to its free end, and as it passes each point moves from its flying place to its hanging place, the cloth losing its twist, its top edge lining up under the keystone as the rod and its body draping down as the hanging height grows. Closing runs the wave back and the cloth takes flight again, then the layer steps behind. About 2.4 s down and 2 s up with sine eases; `?pace=1.5` on the address slows every beat for tuning. `curtain.js` runs the overlay and the choreography and no longer has a cloth of its own; the overlay's white veil went with it (the page's own blur and dim do that work). One canvas, one renderer, one grid (200 by 48) that is long and narrow when flying and wide and tall when hanging; the hanging shape is placed from the page's pixels (`setHang`). Stage two, if wanted: the pull cord (drag the keystone to draw the curtain by hand) and the page tilting back in depth.

## Version 2: the kiosk (branch `v2/main`)

The first version 2 iteration is the kiosk, the root page `index.html` on `v2/main` and `kiosk.html` on `v3/`: the community model alone, full screen, telling its six stories on a loop for an unattended screen. Nothing else from the site is on the page. On `v2/` the full site moved to `site.html`; on `v3/` it is back at the root.

- **The loop.** When the model is ready the kiosk rests on the opening view (threads shown) for `rest` seconds, then steps into each of the six places in turn exactly as the site's tour does (door glide, dive film, captions read by the narrator, end card, exit film), comes back to the opening view, rests, and goes round again. Six dots along the bottom show where the loop is; the current story is red, the told ones darker. The data center is approached under the sky, as on the site.
- **A visitor.** A touch on the model, a label, or the arrow keys hands the kiosk to the visitor: the loop stops, the dots clear, and they can turn the model and step into any building (the room's Leave, Next, Pause and Skip work as on the site; the end card offers only Leave, there being no story pages). After `idle` seconds without a touch the kiosk leaves any open room and starts the loop again from the opening view. Pause from the room's bar also resumes after `idle` seconds. The pointer hides after three seconds still.
- **Sound.** The theme loops under everything and ducks while the narrator speaks. Browsers refuse sound before a touch unless told otherwise, so the kiosk tries, and if refused shows a red "Tap for sound" chip; the first touch anywhere frees it. The mute control at bottom right silences both. For a true unattended kiosk launch Chrome with autoplay allowed, for example `chrome --kiosk --autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required --noerrdialogs --disable-session-crashed-bubble "https://<host>/"`, and sound starts on its own.
- **Address options.** `?rest=10` (seconds on the opening view, default 10, minimum 2), `?idle=45` (seconds before the loop resumes, default 45, minimum 5), `?motion=off` (cuts instead of glides). The visitor's system reduced-motion setting is deliberately ignored on a kiosk.
- **Without WebGL** the still of the community shows with a note; the loop needs the live model.
- **Files:** `impact/index.html` (the kiosk; the full site is `impact/site.html`), `impact/scripts/kiosk.js` (the loop; reuses `room.js`, `scene/city.js` and the journey steps), `impact/styles/kiosk.css`; the page is a build entry in `vite.config.js`. Checks: five in the browser suite (loop starts alone, first story and first dot, a visitor holds it, it resumes, no missing assets). `window.__kiosk` is exposed for the checks.
- **Not yet:** a sleeping screen between loops, a QR code to the full site, a visitor-facing hint on first touch, analytics, a watchdog reload after many hours.

## What was built

A single page in `impact/`, built with Vite, Three.js, GSAP ScrollTrigger and Lenis. Light, calm and Apple-like: generous white space, Inter Tight, one saturated colour (Equinix red) for the thread and small accents, amber for recovered heat.

- **Hero:** "Infrastructure for human possibility." rises into place over white, with the lede and two actions. Below it the community appears as a framed product shot that opens to full bleed as you scroll.
- **The community, yours to explore:** a real 3D model of seven buildings (hospital, research centre, learning centre, homes, high street shops, aquatic centre, Equinix data center) on a white plinth, framed as a product shot in the flow of the page. There are no fixed stops any more (changed on `claude/enter-the-story` at your request): drag to turn the model, click it and then scroll to zoom (scrolling past it without a click still scrolls the page), two fingers on a phone, the arrow keys and plus and minus from the keyboard, and a reset control in the bar that returns to the opening view. A click on any building or its name steps inside (see below). The threads from the data center stay drawn; the aquatic centre's is amber. From the opening view, three headline figures sit as quiet pills and an opening card explains the controls; both give way once you start exploring.
- **Play the tour:** the Play control (in the bar and on the opening card) now takes you inside. For each of the six places in turn it glides to the door, dives in, lets the narrator read the three captions, rests a moment on the end card, plays you out and moves on; about 45 seconds a place, four and a half minutes in all, then back to the opening view with Play again. The calm corporate theme (piano, strings, soft pad) runs underneath and ducks while the narrator speaks. Pause, in the bar or in the room's bar, holds the tour where it is, even mid-film, so you can look around the panorama; Resume carries on. Skip moves to the next place. Leave, Escape, the end card's story link or a hand on the model ends the tour and hands the model back. The next place's dive film is fetched while the current story plays. The mute control silences both music and narrator and is deliberately not remembered between visits. Sound only starts from a click, never on its own.
- **The technique** follows the reference video ("Stay a little longer"): a live 3D model that reacts to cursor and input, reframed per stop, rather than a film. Only the technique was taken; the design is our own.
- **Fallbacks:** a rendered still of the community shows until the model is ready and wherever WebGL is unavailable, with the place names listed so every room is still reachable. With reduced motion or "Pause motion" the camera cuts instead of travelling, there is no orbit, and the controls still work.
- **Statement:** "Equinix is not a visitor in these places…" lights up word by word.
- **Five chapters:** headline, moment, a rounded film, then the story with the connection, the two sets of contributions and the scoped figure, plus links to the other stories in the chapter.
- **In numbers:** four headline figures that count up, each with scope and source. The prefix and suffix (the $, M and +) sit as smaller red marks so the numerals never overflow, and a short thread draws across each tile as it arrives, carrying a travelling bead (amber for heat).
- **Five promises:** a numbered list in large type beside a sticky panel. The promise at the reading line is current; its panel shows a tall photograph with the proof set large over the quiet sky at the top: a headline in a few words, the proof sentence, the kind of evidence, the place and the source. Clicking a promise scrolls to it. The five photographs were generated for this page in one editorial documentary style (low October sun, a small red detail in each) and are captioned as illustrative. On narrow screens and without motion the panel gives way to each promise carrying its photograph and proof inline. The thread that an earlier version drew down the list is gone.
- **Training:** one compact row of three cards (practise with the coach, downloads folded into a details list with one live link, Engage) until the library exists; the four role plays are buttons that open the coach.
- **Stories:** the theatre. The five chapters share one screen: an index on the left, the chosen chapter's film playing on the right with its headline and moment, and the lead story's line beneath it (title, who and where, Open the story, the chapter's other stories, one figure). Every five seconds the next chapter takes over, with a thin red bar under the chosen row showing the dwell; choosing a chapter, by click or by a `#/chapter/<topic>` link, stops the rotation for good on that visit. A pointer resting on the film holds the current chapter. Only the chosen chapter's film plays and the next one is warmed. Module `impact/scripts/theatre.js`, markup from `chaptersHTML` in `render.js`, styles under "Stories: the theatre". The section reads as one rectangle: a compact head sits above the index at the left, the film at the right stretches to exactly the left column's height (cropping the footage's sides a little), and the lead line sits beneath the film. On short screens the index rows tighten so it still fits. The figure beside the lead is a compact column without its scope line (the scope stays in the dialog). On phones the index becomes a row of chips under the film. This took the page from 24 screens to about 14 at 1440 by 900; the old five full chapters were ten screens on their own.
- **People:** the people line. Six colleagues (operations, volunteer, cooling, energy, Pathways coach, heat export) stand in one full-width row over the red fabric (2k portraits, full arms in frame, cut out with rembg, `scripts/gen_people_hi.sh` and `scripts/people_hi_assets.py`); the one you look at steps forward and a horizontal story panel beneath the line changes with them (role, headline, one figure, three lines of the story; click for the whole story and its sources). Shared module `impact/scripts/people-line.js` and stylesheet `impact/styles/people.css`; the prototype page `prototypes/people.html` uses the same module with a backdrop switch (fabric, corridor, plain). The row is sized so all six fit inside the stage and the drift is clamped so nobody at the ends is cut off; the stage has no background or frame of its own, so the fabric shows between and behind the people. Each colleague has a spoken line (Andy, eleven_v4, six lines, 1,064 ElevenLabs credits, recorded under `voice` in the manifest as `voice-people-*`; sources in `media-src/voice/people-*.mp3` stay out of git, web copies mono 96 kb/s at -18 LUFS); the Listen button on the story panel plays it, and appears only when the file exists. The leadership quote sits beneath the line.
- **Training and resources:** a library in the making. Downloadable assets (story deck, five-promises one-pager, the impact story PDFs, an image kit) sit as labelled placeholders until each file is approved, with the Sustainability Report as the one live link. Four role-play scenarios on how to talk about Equinix community impact (a customer, a neighbour, a student, a journalist), each tied to a story on the page and each a placeholder for its Yoodli exercise. An Engage card leads to the Engage page.

It is the one place the site reaches outside its own host; sign-in is Microsoft's. ## The red fabric (branch `claude/red-fabric`)

An experiment you asked for, on its own branch so it can be compared and merged or dropped.

- A length of red satin flies in a slow wind behind the whole page. The cloth is built along a sweeping centreline that rises, dives and turns through space, so the whole length folds over like silk rather than rippling like a ribbon; its width rolls about that line so both faces show, the edges cup toward the face, and small ripples ride on top with a gust that swells and fades.

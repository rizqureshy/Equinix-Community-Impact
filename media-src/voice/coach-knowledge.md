# Equinix community impact: what the site says

This is the complete factual content of the internal "Infrastructure for human possibility" site. Every figure carries its scope and period. Nothing outside this document should be stated as fact.

## The five promises (the Community Principles)

Equinix has made five promises to the places it calls home, reported against every year.
- **Public infrastructure investment.** Promise: Invest in roads, paths and public services around our sites, and fund the power and water infrastructure our sites need. Proof: A public bike path and land for a future fire station in Minooka, Illinois. (Site example; Minooka, Illinois.)
- **Power responsibility.** Promise: Pay our full share of grid costs so our demand does not raise residents’ bills. Proof: A 10-year transmission agreement with ComEd that the utility described as a model. (Agreement; With ComEd.)
- **Jobs and skills development.** Promise: Create quality jobs and educational pathways for tomorrow’s workforce. Proof: School partnerships through Pathways to Tech, starting in Ashburn and Dallas. (Programme; Ashburn and Dallas.)
- **Natural resource protection.** Promise: Use water and land carefully and report transparently. Proof: Avoiding water-based cooling at new sites in water-stressed areas. (Design choice; New sites in water-stressed areas.)
- **Bridge the digital divide.** Promise: Expand access to connectivity, skills and careers. Proof: 28 WeAreEquinix teams supported 31 local digital inclusion nonprofits in 2025. (Result; Employee teams, 2025.)

- 01 Hospital (Care): A radiologist opens a patient’s scan. Somewhere, a family is waiting for what it shows. 3verest runs sovereign healthcare clouds from Equinix facilities so radiologists can retrieve medical images in under a second while patient data stays within its jurisdiction.
- 02 Research centre (Discovery): In Sydney, researchers compare one child’s cancer with cases studied on the other side of the world. Children's Cancer Institute in Sydney uses Equinix Fabric to connect its precision medicine data directly with cloud providers and research collaborators overseas.
- 03 Learning centre (Opportunity): A student walks into a data center for the first time and asks how it all works. Pathways to Tech opens Equinix data centers to students aged 14 to 18 through classroom talks, facility tours and education days led by employee volunteers.
- 04 Homes and high street (Community): In Minooka, Illinois, a public bike path runs along the data center site, and land is being donated for a future fire station. - 05 Aquatic centre (Responsible growth): Early swimmers in Saint-Denis are in water warmed, in part, by heat that once left a data center unused. Equinix PA10 in Saint-Denis, Paris, recovers heat from customer servers for the local heat network that supplies the Olympic Aquatics Centre and homes nearby.
- 06 Equinix data center (Every thread): Private connections for care and research. Heat recovered for neighbours. People who keep it running, and who volunteer down the road. A data center is part of its community, not apart from it.

## How to speak about the Equinix role

Equinix does not deliver the care, the research or the teaching. It provides secure, local, well-connected infrastructure: data center space, private interconnection, and in one case recovered heat. The hospitals, researchers, schools and partners do the work. Never say Equinix "powers hospitals" or "saves lives".

## Stories

### Care

#### Medical images, ready when the care team needs them
Organisation: 3verest. Location: 21 regions worldwide, with the U.K. NHS among its users. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: 3verest runs sovereign healthcare clouds from Equinix facilities so radiologists can retrieve medical images in under a second while patient data stays within its jurisdiction.
Human context: Radiologists read the images that decide what happens next for a patient. When retrieval is slow, a diagnosis waits with it.
Challenge: Medical images are large, heavily regulated and kept for decades, yet clinicians need them in seconds. Healthcare operators also need proof that data, backups and AI inference stay within their jurisdiction.
What Equinix provides: In-country data center space as the physical anchor, carrier-neutral interconnection for private connections between care providers, medical device platforms and clouds, and Equinix Fabric Geo Zones that keep traffic inside defined geographic boundaries, even during failover.
What the partner does: 3verest designs and operates the sovereign healthcare clouds, including imaging archives and governed AI inference, offered through medical device partners such as Agfa, GE HealthCare and Philips.
Outcome: Radiologists working on 3verest clouds can retrieve images in under a second, which 3verest reports as 28 times faster than the minutes seen on general public clouds.
Figure: Under 1 second (to retrieve a medical image). Scope: Reported by 3verest for its own platform, compared with general public clouds. Specific to this customer example. Period: Not stated in source.
The connection, in one line: Equinix does not deliver the care. It provides the secure, local and directly connected foundation that lets 3verest keep critical medical data close, fast and governed.
Quote: "Every slow image retrieval is a radiologist waiting." (Scott Crawford, CEO, 3verest, in the Equinix customer story)
Conversation starter: Ask a healthcare customer where their imaging data lives and how quickly clinicians can reach it.
Sources: S1: 3verest speeds medical image access 28x with Equinix (Equinix customer story)

#### Faster AI development for earlier detection
Organisation: Harrison.ai. Location: Australia. Timeframe: historical. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Harrison.ai develops AI that helps clinicians read chest X-rays, building its models on NVIDIA infrastructure hosted in an Equinix data center.
Human context: Clinicians reading chest X-rays look for early signs of cancer and other critical illness, often under time pressure.
Challenge: Training clinical AI models takes large amounts of compute close to sensitive data.
What Equinix provides: A data center home for private AI infrastructure, close to the networks and partners Harrison.ai depends on.
What the partner does: Harrison.ai builds and validates the AI solutions and works with clinicians who make the diagnoses. NVIDIA provides the AI infrastructure.
Outcome: Equinix reports that the private AI deployment gave Harrison.ai 8x the data processing capability and reduced model training from months to days.
Figure: Months to days (model training time). Scope: As reported by Equinix for Harrison.ai’s private AI deployment. Clinical accuracy claims belong to Harrison.ai and are not restated here. Period: Reported March 2025.
The connection, in one line: The clinical progress is Harrison.ai’s. Equinix provides a well-connected place for the infrastructure that powers its development work.
Conversation starter: Healthcare AI teams often need compute next to sensitive data. This is a short, specific example to share.
Sources: S2b: How life science and healthcare organizations can get AI-ready (Equinix blog)

### Discovery

#### Comparing notes across the world for every child with cancer
Organisation: Children's Cancer Institute. Location: Sydney, Australia, with collaborators in Philadelphia, Memphis and Heidelberg. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Children's Cancer Institute in Sydney uses Equinix Fabric to connect its precision medicine data directly with cloud providers and research collaborators overseas.
Human context: Every childhood cancer is different, and treatments developed for adults often do not translate. Researchers measure as much as they can about each case to find what will help.
Challenge: That precision medicine approach creates terabytes of data, and the most useful patterns appear only when it is compared with data from research centres in other countries. In-house computing could not scale to that, and the data needed protecting.
What Equinix provides: Equinix Fabric gives the Institute a direct, private connection into its cloud providers and to collaborators worldwide, without the carrier costs usually involved in building that connectivity.
What the partner does: The Institute’s researchers lead the science, from generating the data to integrating it with partner centres and pursuing new treatments.
Outcome: Data generated in Sydney can be compared directly with data from Philadelphia, Memphis and Heidelberg in the same cloud environment.
The connection, in one line: The discoveries belong to the researchers. Equinix helps by shortening the distance between their data and the people they need to compare it with.
Quote: "Equinix allows us to have a direct, secure connection into our cloud providers and fundamentally connect globally to our collaborators." (Emilio Caspanello, ICT Manager, Children's Cancer Institute, in the Equinix customer story)
Conversation starter: Research customers often collaborate across borders. Ask how they move large datasets between partners today.
Sources: S2: Children's Cancer Institute relies on Equinix to connect its data directly with global partners (Equinix customer story); S2b: How life science and healthcare organizations can get AI-ready (Equinix blog)

#### From a school tour to a technician’s badge
Organisation: Equinix employees and the Equinix Foundation. Location: Now scaling to every Equinix location. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Pathways to Tech opens Equinix data centers to students aged 14 to 18 through classroom talks, facility tours and education days led by employee volunteers.
Human context: Kayla first explored tech careers on a Pathways to Tech data center tour as a resident at i.c. stars, an Equinix Foundation partner in Chicago. Within months she had an IBX internship. Today she is a full-time Equinix Data Center Technician on the Chicago team.
Challenge: The work that keeps data centers running is largely hidden, so most young people never consider it as a career, or know how to start.
What Equinix provides: Employee volunteers lead classroom talks, IBX tours and hands-on education days, and the programme offers a structured path into Equinix internships, apprenticeships and early-career roles. School partnerships began in Ashburn, Virginia, and Dallas, Texas.
What the partner does: Schools and youth organisations, including Foundation partners such as i.c. stars, bring students and support them into training and work.
Outcome: After a two-year pilot that reached nearly 2,000 students across the Americas and Asia-Pacific, Pathways to Tech is scaling to all Equinix locations worldwide.
Figure: Nearly 2,000 (students reached in the two-year pilot). Scope: Two-year pilot in select communities across the Americas and Asia-Pacific, as reported in the 2025 Sustainability Report and Equinix blog. Period: Pilot completed by 2025.
The connection, in one line: This is Equinix acting directly: our own people opening our own facilities, so students can picture themselves working there.
Conversation starter: Ask local colleagues whether a nearby school could join Pathways to Tech, or whether a customer would like to co-host.
Sources: S4c: Pathways to Tech: connecting ambitious youth to data center and tech careers (Equinix Foundation); S6: 2025 Sustainability Report (Equinix); S8: The heartbeat of the digital world runs on people (Equinix blog); S7b: We're not just building data centers. #### An industry-wide answer to the technician shortage
Organisation: Equinix Foundation with Generation and coalition partners. Location: Brazil first, with wider expansion planned. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: The Equinix Foundation and Generation launched the Global Data Center Technician Training Coalition, starting in Brazil with co-funding from ODATA, Cisco and Vertiv.
Human context: For an unemployed young adult, a technician course with a job at the end can change the direction of a life.
Challenge: The Uptime Institute estimates the data center industry is short of about 2.4 million workers, a problem too big for one company to solve.
What Equinix provides: The Foundation created the coalition and helps fund it, alongside Equinix apprenticeships and new Learning Labs in Dallas, Paris and Singapore.
What the partner does: Generation, a global nonprofit that trains unemployed young people for high-demand jobs, delivers the training. ODATA, Cisco Systems and Vertiv co-fund the first programme.
Outcome: Initial cohorts in Brazil were planned to begin in June 2026, the starting point for a broader global expansion.
The connection, in one line: Generation delivers the outcome. Equinix convened the coalition so the whole industry can grow the workforce together.
Quote: "Your gender doesn’t define what you can do with your life." (Holly Byrne Nugent, Equinix Technician Apprentice, quoted on the Equinix blog)
Conversation starter: Customers hiring for operations face the same shortage. The coalition invites co-funding partners.
Sources: S8: The heartbeat of the digital world runs on people (Equinix blog)

### Community

#### A neighbour that plans to stay for decades
Organisation: Equinix with local governments, utilities and community leaders. Location: Minooka, Illinois; San Jose, California; and U.S. communities where Equinix operates. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Equinix’s U.S. Community Principles set out what neighbours can expect: paying our full share of grid costs, investing in public infrastructure, local jobs and skills, careful use of resources and digital inclusion.
Human context: In Minooka, Illinois, local leaders told Equinix what their community needed. The answer included a public bike path along the site and land for a future fire station.
Challenge: Data centers use power, water and land, and AI is accelerating demand. Communities rightly ask who pays for grid upgrades and what a new neighbour will give back.
What Equinix provides: At the Minooka campus, Equinix is contributing $17M in public infrastructure, including roads, traffic signals and multi-use paths. Outcome: In 2025, Equinix spent more than $95 million with certified small, veteran-owned and other qualified businesses through inclusive procurement in the U.S.
Figure: $95M+ (with small and inclusive U.S. businesses). Scope: U.S. inclusive procurement with certified and qualified businesses in calendar year 2025, as reported in Equinix’s U.S. Community Principles. Period: 2025.
The connection, in one line: Here the thread is not a cable. It is the long-term relationship between a data center and the town around it: roads, paths, power, taxes and local work.
Quote: "We’re not just building data centers. We’re building communities." (Arquelle Shaw, President of the Americas, Equinix, June 2026)
Conversation starter: When a customer or community asks what a data center means for them, the five U.S. Community Principles give a specific, sourced answer.

### Responsible growth

#### Warm water for a neighbourhood pool in Saint-Denis
Organisation: Equinix PA10 with the local heat network operator. Location: Saint-Denis, Paris, France. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Equinix PA10 in Saint-Denis, Paris, recovers heat from customer servers for the local heat network that supplies the Olympic Aquatics Centre and homes nearby.
Human context: Swimmers, schools and local clubs use the Aquatic Centre, which hosted events at the Paris 2024 Olympics.
Challenge: Servers produce heat that is usually released into the air. The neighbourhood around them needs heat for water and homes.
What Equinix provides: PA10 opened in 2023 and was designed to recover and export heat from customer equipment. Heat is recovered at about 28°C, raised to 65°C by heat pumps and passed through a heat exchanger to the heat network operator.
What the partner does: The heat network operator distributes the heat to the Olympic Aquatics Centre and the surrounding Plaine Saulnier neighbourhood.
Outcome: Equinix connected PA10 to the heat network supplying the Aquatics Centre in 2024. Its excess heat is expected to warm more than 1,000 homes nearby, and Equinix describes the heat as provided free of charge for 15 years.
Figure: 1,000+ (homes expected to be warmed). Scope: Expected reach of PA10 heat export, as stated by Equinix in April 2025. Specific to this site. Period: From 2024.
The connection, in one line: Here the thread is warm, not digital: heat leaving a data center and arriving as warm water in a public pool.
Quote: "Our heat export initiative demonstrates the power of collective community action to support a move toward a circular economy." (Noah Nkonge, Heat Export Lead, Equinix, quoted by Data Center Knowledge, July 2024)
Conversation starter: Customers with sustainability goals often ask what happens to data center heat. PA10 is a specific, named example.
Sources: S3: The hidden benefit of data centers: warming communities, not just servers (Equinix blog); S3b: Paris 2024: Excess data center heat used to warm Olympic swimming pools (Data Center Knowledge); S3c: Sustainably heating the Paris 2024 Olympic pool (Equinix video page)

#### Heat for homes, offices and two community pools in Markham
Organisation: Equinix TR5 with Markham District Energy. Location: Markham, Ontario, Canada. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Equinix TR5 exports residual heat to Markham District Energy, the city-owned utility that warms buildings, condo hot water and two community centre pools.
Human context: Markham created its district energy utility after the 1998 ice storm, so families would stay warm even if the electricity grid failed.
Challenge: A fast-growing city needs reliable, efficient heat and has a goal of net zero emissions by 2050.
What Equinix provides: Since late 2023, Equinix TR5 has exported residual heat into the Markham District Energy system through its heat pump project. Equinix seeks no income from the heat.
What the partner does: Markham District Energy distributes the heat to over 14 million square feet of mixed-use development, including condo hot water and two swimming pools in community centres.
Outcome: The heat supports Markham’s Municipal Energy Plan. In summer there is sometimes more heat than the community can use; the aim is to recover all of it as more buildings connect.
Figure: 14M sq ft (of mixed-use buildings served by the network). Scope: Floor area served by the Markham District Energy system that TR5 contributes to, as described by Equinix in April 2025. TR5 is one of its heat sources. Period: From late 2023.
The connection, in one line: Markham District Energy delivers the warmth. Equinix contributes heat that would otherwise go to waste.
Quote: "We’re using the MDE system to reduce emissions in the community and provide efficient heating and cooling to buildings in our City." (Peter Ronson, Chief Operating Officer, Markham District Energy, on the Equinix blog)
Conversation starter: Heat export now runs at five Equinix data centers. Markham shows how it fits a city’s own energy plan.
Sources: S3: The hidden benefit of data centers: warming communities, not just servers (Equinix blog); S6: 2025 Sustainability Report (Equinix)

#### Running facilities more efficiently, year after year
Organisation: Equinix. Location: Global retail IBX portfolio. Timeframe: current. Verification: sourced, last checked 2026-10-21.

Summary: Equinix reports its 2025 performance, an average annual PUE of 1.37 and 96% renewable energy coverage across retail IBX data centers, and has dated commitments for what comes next.
Human context: Every customer workload and every community connection depends on facilities that use energy and water carefully.
Challenge: Demand for digital infrastructure, including AI, is rising. Growth needs to come with efficiency, careful water use and a credible path to lower emissions.
What Equinix provides: Energy efficiency investment ($36M in 2025), clean and renewable energy purchases, on-site fuel cells, and at new sites, avoiding water-based cooling in water-stressed areas. Recovered heat from five data centers was exported to local communities as of 2025.
What the partner does: Energy suppliers, utilities and technology partners contribute, and more than 600 customers use Green Power Reports for their own reporting.
Outcome: In 2025, Equinix reported an average annual PUE of 1.37 and 96% renewable energy coverage across retail IBX data centers, the eighth year in a row above 90% globally.
Figure: 1.37 (average annual PUE in 2025). Scope: Equinix reported average for 2025. Renewable energy coverage uses Equinix’s published definition across retail IBX data centers and does not mean continuous direct renewable supply at every site. Period: 1 January to 31 December 2025.
The connection, in one line: Responsibility here is operational: measured practices, reported every year, with dated commitments for the future.
Conversation starter: Point customers to Green Power Reports and the annual Sustainability Report for scoped, dated figures.
Sources: S5: Environmental sustainability (Equinix); S6: 2025 Sustainability Report (Equinix); S7: Our U.S. community principles (Equinix Together)

Sketch round 1 · 25 October 2026

Four ways to learn a story well enough to tell it.

Live sketches, not mock-ups. Each one runs on the same eleven stories and nine sourced figures as the main page, costs nothing to run, and tests a different kind of practice: delivering, recalling, explaining aloud, and judging. Try all four, then pick one to build properly.

  1. Pitch modeNinety seconds on one chapter, beat by beat, with a one-page leave-behind at the end.
  2. Scope checkA recall drill: every figure must travel with its scope, period and source. Pick the right ones.
  3. Teach-backExplain a story aloud in sixty seconds. The page listens and scores it the way the coach does.
  4. Choose the replyA customer, a neighbour, a student and a journalist. Three turns each. Every reply has a consequence.
1

Pitch mode

Delivering. Choose a chapter, press Pitch, and say each beat as it arrives. Eighteen seconds a beat, five beats, ninety seconds. Where a figure is spoken, the caveat to say with it sits underneath. At the end: a leave-behind you can copy or print.

        Scope check

        Recalling. The house rule is that a figure never travels alone. Each card shows one of the nine figures; choose the scope, the period and the source that belong to it. The wrong answers are real too, they just belong to other figures. Missed cards come back.

        Explaining aloud. Pick a story and tell it in sixty seconds as if to a colleague. Where the browser can listen, it transcribes as you speak; otherwise type. The score checks the same five things the coach does: who, the figure, its scope or period, a source, and Equinix as infrastructure with the partner doing the work. Nothing leaves this page.

            4

            Choose the reply

            Judging. Four people, three turns each. Read what they say, choose a reply, and watch how they react. Every reply is graded against the house rules, and the notes say why. Finish a conversation and the coach is one click away for the live version.

            Debrief

              Practise it live with the coach

              If this one is chosen: branches that remember earlier replies, the other person voiced by the narrator, a free-text reply option scored by the coach, and new conversations authored from any story in minutes.
